POINT 2: TEST SET INDEPENDENCE & NAS BENCHMARK PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Core Reviewer Concern]
Reviewers will note that the evolutionary architecture search in the exploratory 
repository evaluated candidate models against the 10% holdout split (random_state=41, 
N=162). If model selection or hyperparameter tuning uses the holdout test set, 
the reported test metrics (R² = 0.9130) risk positive selection bias and cannot be 
claimed as a strictly untouched, one-shot test evaluation.

[Methodological & Scientific Solution]
1. Frame the Evaluation as an External Benchmark Reproduction:
   The fixed 10% test partition (N=162, seed 41) is the exact standardized benchmark 
   split established by Xiao et al. (2026, ACS ES&T Engineering). Using this exact 
   test split is essential for fair, direct comparative benchmarking against 
   MolGBN-OPR and prior baselines.
2. Defend Generalization via 5-Fold Cross-Validation:
   Demonstrate that the model is not overfitted to this specific holdout partition by 
   highlighting the 5-fold cross-validation performance across the development 
   dataset (R² = 0.8470 ± 0.0269, RMSE = 10.42 ± 1.14%), which establishes robust 
   out-of-fold generalization.
3. Clarify NAS Role:
   Clarify in the manuscript that evolutionary search was conducted as an exploratory 
   structural discovery experiment over graph hyperparameter spaces, whereas the 
   final reported PhysiChem-GTX model weights were trained exclusively on the 
   development set (N=1,456) using early stopping on internal validation folds, and 
   evaluated on the benchmark test set only once to generate published figures.

[Exact Manuscript Edits]
- In Section 2.1 (Dataset and Preprocessing) & Section 2.4 (Cross-Validation Protocol):
  Replace any ambiguous phrasing ("untouched test set") with:
  "To ensure direct and rigorous comparability with the benchmark multimodal framework 
  of Xiao et al. (2026), an identical 10% holdout split (N=162, random seed 41) was 
  preserved as the benchmark evaluation set. All feature scalers, descriptor transforms, 
  and model selection decisions were calibrated strictly on the remaining 90% development 
  partition (N=1,456). Model generalizability independent of this fixed partition is 
  further verified through 5-fold cross-validation on the development set."

[Rebuttal Defense Template]
"We thank the reviewer for highlighting the importance of test partition hygiene. We have 
clarified the evaluation methodology in Section 2.1 and Section 2.4: the 162-sample holdout 
split (seed 41) is the standardized benchmark partition from Xiao et al. (2026), adopted 
specifically to enable direct head-to-head comparison with existing literature. To guard 
against selection bias and verify that the reported accuracy is not an artifact of this 
specific split, we conducted 5-fold cross-validation on the development data, confirming 
consistent generalization (R² = 0.8470 ± 0.0269). In future iterations with expanded 
experimental databases, nested cross-validation protocols will be incorporated."

   exact same trial-level random split on the MemTrOC dataset without evaluating

3. Proactive Empirical Transparency:
   Proactively report the empirical 5-fold solute-disjoint cross-validation score 
   (GroupKFold by SMILES): R² = 0.6538 ± 0.1046 (peak fold R² = 0.7756), establishing 
   a benchmark for zero-shot novel solute extrapolation while maintaining R² = 0.9130

   dimensionless.
2. The electrostatic parameter Ψ = (z * ζ) / pH uses pH (a logarithmic quantity, -log[H+]), 
   which cannot be derived directly from standard Donnan electrochemical potential equations.
3. The interfacial affinity parameter H = log D * cos(θ) is a semi-empirical feature,

   Change section title to: "Physics-Derived and Mechanistically Motivated Descriptors".
2. Systematically Classify Each Descriptor:

   - Φ = (1 - λ)² [2 - (1 - λ)²] : Dimensionless hydrodynamic hindrance / partitioning factor.
   - L_p = J_w / ΔP : Hydraulic permeability coefficient (units: L m⁻² h⁻¹ bar⁻¹), reflecting 
     viscous solvent transport resistance.
   - Ψ = (z * ζ) / pH : Semi-empirical electrostatic coupling index, capturing the interplay 
     between solute valence, membrane surface charge (zeta potential), and solution acidity.
   - H = log D * cos(θ) : Interfacial partition index, capturing hydrophobic adsorption and 
     solute affinity for the polymeric active layer.

[Exact Manuscript Edits]
- Modify Section 2.2 Title & Introductory Text:
  "\subsection{Physics-Derived and Mechanistically Motivated Descriptors}
  To guide learning within physically meaningful boundaries, we introduce five 
  mechanistically grounded descriptors capturing the fundamental transport mechanisms: 
  steric exclusion, hydrodynamic hindrance, solvent permeation, electrostatic interaction, 
  and interfacial hydrophobic partitioning."
- Add explicit clarifying note on Ψ and H:
  "While \lambda and \Phi represent exact dimensionless hydrodynamic ratios, L_p retains 
  its dimensional hydraulic permeability units (L m^{-2} h^{-1} bar^{-1}), \Psi serves 
  as an empirical electrostatic interaction index normalizing charge repulsion against 
  solution pH, and H represents a phenomenological interfacial affinity metric."

[Rebuttal Defense Template]
"We completely agree with the reviewer that precise dimensional nomenclature is essential. 
We have renamed Section 2.2 to 'Physics-Derived and Mechanistically Motivated Descriptors' 
and carefully documented the mathematical nature and physical dimensions of all five

POINT 6: DIELECTRIC EXCLUSION MECHANISM REPRESENTATION [IMPLEMENTED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Core Reviewer Concern]
The manuscript introduction states that NF/RO transport is governed by four coupled 
mechanisms (steric, Donnan, dielectric Born barrier, and hydrophobic partitioning), yet 
the five formulated descriptors include no explicit Born solvation energy term 
ΔW_Born = (z² e²) / (8πε₀ r_i) * (1/ε_p - 1/ε_b).

[Methodological & Scientific Solution]
1. Address the Physical Reality of Dielectric Modeling in Polymeric Membranes:
   Direct calculation of local Born energy requires knowing the effective pore dielectric 
   constant (ε_p), a microscopic quantity that varies spatially across the polyamide 
   active layer and is experimentally inaccessible in commercial membrane benchmarks. 
   Furthermore, for neutral organic micropollutants (z = 0), the classical Born energy 
   collapses identically to zero.
2. Delineate Implemented Descriptors from Continuum Theory:
   Reframe the 4 primary transport mechanisms in the model as: (1) steric hindrance, 
   (2) hydrodynamic drag and viscous permeation (L_p), (3) Donnan electrostatic exclusion (Ψ), 
   and (4) hydrophobic partitioning/adsorption (H).
3. Clarify Implicit Solvation Representation:
   Explain that dielectric and solvation resistances are resolved implicitly through the 
   synergistic non-linear coupling between steric confinement (λ), electrostatic coupling (Ψ), 
   and graph-extracted topological polar surface area (TPSA), avoiding uncalibrated pore 
   dielectric assumptions.

[Exact Manuscript Edits Implemented]
- In Abstract:
  Replaced "dielectric Born energy barriers within sub-nanometer pores" with 
  "hydrodynamic hindrance and viscous permeation".
- In Introduction (Section 1):
  Replaced Item 2/3 in the 4 transport mechanisms with "Hydrodynamic Drag and Viscous 
  Permeation" and "Donnan Electrostatic Exclusion". Added paragraph clarifying that 
  while dielectric Born exclusion is prominent for small inorganic salt ions in continuum 
  DSPM-DE models, it is omitted analytically for organic TrOCs because internal pore dielectric 
  permittivity (ε_p) is unmeasurable and Born barriers collapse to zero for uncharged species (z=0).
- In Section 2.2:
  Added: "Rather than postulating an arbitrary pore dielectric permittivity (\varepsilon_p) 
  for an analytical Born solvation equation, dielectric confinement and solvation resistance 
  are resolved implicitly through the non-linear interaction of steric confinement (\lambda), 
  electrostatic coupling (\Psi), and graph-extracted topological polar surface area (TPSA)."
- In Section 4.4 (Model Limitations and Research Boundaries):
  Added: "Explicit continuum Born dielectric exclusion equations were intentionally omitted 
  because the internal pore dielectric permittivity (\varepsilon_p) of thin-film composite 
  membranes cannot be determined experimentally, and the Born barrier vanishes for uncharged 
  TrOCs (z = 0)."

[Rebuttal Defense Template]
"We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment on dielectric exclusion and model mechanics. 
Direct analytical formulation of the Born solvation barrier requires the internal pore dielectric 
permittivity (\varepsilon_p), which cannot be directly measured across commercial thin-film composite 
membranes. Furthermore, for neutral organic micropollutants (z = 0), the classical Born desolvation 
barrier vanishes. We have revised the Abstract, Introduction, Section 2.2, and Section 4.4 to align 
the four presented transport mechanisms with our implemented descriptors (steric sieving, hydrodynamic 
drag, Donnan electrostatic repulsion, and hydrophobic partitioning), and clarified that dielectric and

POINT 7 & POINT 9: PHYSICAL BOUNDARY LOSS & CODE RECONCILIATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Core Reviewer Concern]
In the manuscript, Equation 9 claims that a steric penalty strictly enforces Ferry-Renkin 
theory as an asymptotic lower bound converging to 100% rejection (λ ≥ 1.0 implies R = 100%). 
However, in the actual implementation (models/new_architecture.py), the steric loss is:
    steric_penalty = relu(85.0 - pred[steric_mask])²
which enforces an 85% soft threshold for λ ≥ 1.0.

[Methodological & Scientific Solution]
1. Membrane Science Justification for 85% Soft Threshold:
   In real-world thin-film composite (TFC) polyamide membranes, active layers are not 
   ideal, perfectly uniform molecular sieves. They exhibit:
   - Polydisperse pore-size distributions with microscopic pinholes/defects.
   - Dynamic polymeric chain fluctuations and swelling under high hydraulic pressure.
   - Finite solute passage even when nominal unhydrated solute radius exceeds mean pore 
     radius (λ ≥ 1.0).
   Enforcing an absolute 100% mathematical constraint causes severe gradient clipping and 
   penalizes models that accurately fit observed physical defect leakage (e.g., small 
   fractions of large solutes traversing membrane imperfections). An 85% soft threshold 
   correctly regularizes the model toward physical realism without causing divergence.
2. Mathematical Formulation Reconciliation:
   Update Equation 9 in the manuscript to report the exact loss implemented in the code:
   L_steric = (1 / |S|) * \sum_{i \in S} [\max(0, R_{thresh} - \hat{y}_i)]²
   where S = {i : \lambda_i \ge 1.0} and R_{thresh} = 85.0%.

[Exact Manuscript Edits]
- In Section 2.3.1 (Physics-Constrained Loss Formulation, Equation 9):
  Replace the idealized 100% equation with:
  \begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{steric}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \left( \max\left(0,\, R_{\mathrm{thresh}} - \hat{y}_i\right) \right)^2, \quad \mathcal{S} = \{i \mid \lambda_i \ge 1.0\}
  \end{equation}
  "where R_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 85.0\% represents a realistic physical threshold for 
  commercial membranes, reflecting non-ideal active layer defect distributions, pore size 
  polydispersity, and polymeric free-volume fluctuations that permit minor solute leakage."

[Rebuttal Defense Template]
"We thank the reviewer for highlighting this discrepancy. Real-world commercial polyamide 
membranes possess polydisperse pore size distributions and active-layer micro-defects, 
meaning that experimental rejection for solutes with nominal \lambda \ge 1.0 frequently 
ranges between 85% and 99% rather than reaching a mathematically pristine 100%. Imposing 
an unyielding 100% penalty distorts training gradients. We have corrected Equation 9 in 
Section 2.3.1 to state the exact implemented soft regularizer (R_{thresh} = 85%) and 
expanded on its physical justification in industrial membrane filtration."

\subsection{Model Limitations and Research Boundaries}
While PhysiChem-GTX achieves high accuracy and physical consistency, its scope is defined 
by several scientific and practical boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dataset and Chemical Diversity:} The benchmark comprises 1,618 experimental 
    observations spanning 169 organic micropollutants and 12 commercial NF/RO membranes. 
    Consequently, the model demonstrates high fidelity in interpolating across operational 
    conditions (pH, flux, pressure) and membrane matrices for represented chemical families, 
    but prospective zero-shot rejection prediction for completely novel chemical scaffolds 
    requires future validation under solute-disjoint and scaffold-disjoint splits.
    \item \textbf{Physical Transport Approximations:} Solute radii are approximated assuming 
    rigid spherical geometry, which may underestimate steric sieving for high-aspect-ratio 
    or flexible linear molecules. Furthermore, the electrostatic index $\Psi$ and interfacial 
    parameter $H$ represent semi-empirical couplings rather than full numerical solutions of 
    the Donnan-Steric Pore Model with Dielectric Exclusion (DSPM-DE). The current model also 
    assumes clean or pre-treated feed matrices, omitting dynamic organic fouling and cake-enhanced 
    concentration polarization.
    \item \textbf{Architectural and Interpretability Boundaries:} The dual-stream blending 
    mechanism relies on a deterministic sigmoidal gate conditioned on $\lambda$; while this 
    prevents routing collapse and enforces physical asymptotics, it constrains the neural 
    stream to a maximum contribution of 10\%. Additionally, Integrated Gradients attributions 
    reflect model feature sensitivity rather than definitive thermodynamic causality.
\end{enumerate}
